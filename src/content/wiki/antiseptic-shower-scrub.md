This anti-septic shower scrub recipe comes from my lovely wife — a tried and trusted blend of simple kitchen ingredients. This paste combines the cleansing power of lemon, the antibacterial properties of neem and tea tree oil, the gentle exfoliation of chickpea flour and red lentils, the soothing moisture of yoghurt, and the anti-inflammatory action of wild turmeric.

<!--more-->

## Ingredients

| Amount                                | Ingredient                                  |
|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| 5–8 Tablespoons (4–5 ice cubes)       | 🍋  Fresh lemon juice                       |
| 1 teaspoon                            | 🌿  Neem powder                             |
| 6 teaspoons                           | 🫘  Chickpea flour (besan)                  |
| 8–10 teaspoons                        | 🥣  Yoghurt (for moisture)                  |
| 8 teaspoons                           | 🟠  Red lentils, ground (for peeling)       |
| 0.25 teaspoon                         | 🟡  Wild turmeric powder (kasthuri manjal)  |
| 10 drops                              | 🫧  Tea tree oil *(mix in only before use)* |

> **Important:** Use only organic source material. If using lemon juice from ice cubes, let them melt completely before mixing. Tea tree oil is added only right before you step into the shower.

## How to Mix

1. Add all dry ingredients (neem powder, chickpea flour, ground red lentils, wild turmeric) to a bowl
2. Add the yoghurt and lemon juice
3. Stir well with a spoon until you get a smooth, spreadable paste
4. **Right before showering**, add the 10 drops of tea tree oil and mix in thoroughly

## How to Use — Full Body Scrub

1. Step into the shower and wet your entire body
2. **Turn off the water**
3. Apply the paste to your full body, gently avoiding the eyes, nose, and mouth when applying to the face
4. Gently scrub the paste onto your skin for **10 to 15 minutes**
5. Wash off with lukewarm water
6. **Do not** apply any other shower gel afterwards
7. As the scrub can dry the skin, apply a moisturising lotion after towelling off

## Second Use — Localised Skin Treatment

For localised skin damage (no open wounds), you can make a thicker paste:

- Mix yoghurt, chickpea flour (gram flour), neem powder, wild turmeric, and a few drops of tea tree oil
- Apply the thick paste to the affected body part
- Leave for **15 to 20 minutes**
- Wash off with lukewarm water

## Ingredient Breakdown

### 🍋 Lemon Juice

Lemon juice is rich in **vitamin C** and **citric acid** — a natural alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) that acts as a chemical exfoliant, sloughing off dead skin cells and unclogging pores. Vitamin C is a potent antioxidant that supports collagen production and helps protect skin from free radical damage. Lemon juice also has natural **antibacterial and antiseptic** properties, making it effective against acne-causing bacteria. Its astringent quality helps control excess oil and can lighten dark spots over time.

### 🌿 Neem Powder

Neem (*Azadirachta indica*) has been used in Ayurvedic medicine for centuries and is sometimes called "nature's pharmacy." The powder retains active compounds like nimbin, nimbidin, and quercetin, which give it powerful **antibacterial, antifungal, and anti-inflammatory** properties. Neem helps fight acne-causing bacteria, soothes irritated or inflamed skin (including conditions like eczema and psoriasis), controls excess oil production, and can lighten acne scars and blemishes over time. It also has antiseptic properties that help prevent skin infections.

### 🫘 Chickpea Flour (Besan)

Chickpea flour — also known as gram flour or besan — is a staple in traditional South Asian skincare. Its fine texture provides **gentle mechanical exfoliation** that buffs away dead skin cells without the harshness of commercial scrubs. Rich in **B vitamins, zinc, and antioxidants**, it supports healthy skin cell turnover, helps absorb excess oil and impurities, and can brighten dull complexions. Chickpea flour acts as a natural cleanser, unclogging pores and helping prevent breakouts while maintaining the skin's natural pH balance.

### 🥣 Yoghurt

Yoghurt is a natural source of **lactic acid** — an alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) that gently dissolves the bonds between dead skin cells, promoting chemical exfoliation. Unlike harsher acids, lactic acid is also a **humectant**, meaning it attracts and retains moisture in the skin, keeping it hydrated during the exfoliation process. Yoghurt also contains **probiotics** that support the skin's natural microbiome, helping to calm inflammation, reduce redness, and strengthen the skin barrier. Its natural fats and proteins provide additional nourishment and moisturisation.

### 🟠 Red Lentils (Ground)

Ground red lentils (masoor dal) serve as the **physical exfoliant** in this scrub. Their coarse texture gently removes dead skin cells and unclogs pores while delivering a concentration of skin-active nutrients — including **vitamins B-complex, C, and E**, as well as **iron, zinc, and magnesium**. Red lentils have antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties that help combat free radicals and soothe irritated skin. In Ayurveda, red lentils are classified as *varnya* (skin-purifying) and have been used to brighten complexion, remove tanning, and improve overall skin texture.

### 🟡 Wild Turmeric (Kasthuri Manjal)

Wild turmeric (*Curcuma aromatica*), also known as kasthuri manjal, is different from culinary turmeric (*Curcuma longa*) — it is not edible, but it is superior for topical skincare and **does not stain the skin yellow**. Its primary active compound, **curcumin**, provides strong **anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and antioxidant** effects. Wild turmeric helps brighten skin tone, reduce pigmentation and dark spots, fight acne-causing bacteria, and soothe conditions like eczema and psoriasis. It also has natural exfoliating properties and helps control excess oil production.

### 🫧 Tea Tree Oil

Tea tree oil (*Melaleuca alternifolia*) is one of the most evidence-backed natural antimicrobials. Its primary active compound, **terpinen-4-ol**, disrupts bacterial cell membranes, providing **broad-spectrum antibacterial, antifungal, and antiseptic** activity. Research shows it is effective against *Propionibacterium acnes* (acne-causing bacteria), *Staphylococcus aureus*, and various fungal organisms. Tea tree oil also has anti-inflammatory properties that reduce redness, swelling, and irritation. Because pure tea tree oil can irritate skin, it should always be diluted — in this recipe it is mixed into the paste just before application.
